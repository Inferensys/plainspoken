# Proof, risk reversal, and CTAs

## Proof formats, strongest first

1. **Named client, before → after, timeframe, scope.** "[Client] cut [task] from [X] to [Y] in [N weeks], after we [scope]." A missing baseline is the most common reason buyers discount a result.
2. **Named quote with a number,** with the person's name and title. Anonymous quotes ("CTO, fintech") are much weaker.
3. **Commercial terms:** price or range, milestone billing, fixed-scope pilots, refund or replacement terms, IP ownership. These are promises the client can enforce, so they read as true.
4. **Public work:** open-source projects, published benchmarks or evals, a playbook, a course, a talk. For technical buyers this often beats logos. Examples: Vstorm's Pydantic partnership and libraries, Test Double maintaining Standard Ruby, thoughtbot's Playbook.
5. **The people:** named engineers or founders, with what they've shipped. Put a name in a CTA ("Email Rich…").
6. **Third-party validation:** analyst ratings, reviews on Clutch or G2, press. NN/g found users distrust praise a company publishes about itself.
7. **Logos.** These only work with outcomes beside them, or for very recognisable names.

Never write a number, certification, partnership or client name that isn't in the fact inventory. Use `[PROOF: …]` placeholders instead.

## Risk reversal for services

Services buyers are afraid of three things: paying for months with nothing to show, getting junior people, and being locked in. Answer each one with a term, not reassurance:
- **Staged engagement:** a paid, fixed-scope discovery or pilot, sometimes credited against the full build.
- **Milestone billing** tied to outputs the client can test.
- **Team approval:** the client interviews the engineers, and replacement terms are written into the contract.
- **Ownership:** the client owns the code, models and evals from day one.
- **Honest exit:** say outright that some engagements end with "don't build this yet".

Guarantee what the firm controls (deliverables, response times, team continuity), not business outcomes it doesn't control.

## CTA intent ladder

Up to 95% of B2B buyers are out of market at any given moment (Dawes, B2B Institute). For them, a page's job is to be remembered and to be useful, not to book a meeting. Match the CTA to the reader's intent:

| Visitor intent | Typical page | Primary CTA | Secondary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learning (low) | Glossary, how-to guide | A useful resource on the same topic: checklist, template, assessment, newsletter that names its audience | Related guide |
| Evaluating (medium) | Comparison, use case, "build vs buy" | Case study for the matching situation, pricing page, process page | Short call |
| Buying (high) | Service page, homepage, pricing | A specific booking: "Book 30 minutes with an engineer" | Non-call route: email, written brief |

Rules:
- One primary goal per page, with at most one secondary (Oli Gardner's attention ratio).
- Don't send high-intent readers back to educational content.
- Don't put a booking button above the answer on a learning page.
- Low-intent offers must be worth having on their own, and should name who they're for. Vstorm's is a newsletter "for people who run agents in production". Thoughtworks has its Technology Radar. Vstorm and Test Double offer a 10–15 minute assessment and free office hours.

## CTA labels

Name the next step, how long it takes, who is in it, and what you leave with. Use the same primary label everywhere on the page.

| Reads human | Reads salesy or empty |
|---|---|
| Book 30 minutes with an engineer | Get a Free AI Consultation |
| Book a 15-min intro call | Book a Free Scoping Call |
| Talk to a human | Get My AI Plan |
| Say hello | start ai journey |
| Email [Name] about your project | Let's work together |
| Prefer to email? | Learn more / Read more (bare) |
| See pricing | Explore Services |
| Read the [specific result] case study | Get started (on a services firm) |

"Free" on a consultation tells the reader a sales call is coming. Specify the meeting instead. Vstorm: "Book 45 minutes with our founders and senior AI engineers."

Put a small line of reassurance next to the button. Use only the facts the client confirms, such as "NDA available", "No prep needed", "You'll talk to the engineers who'd do the work", or "Reply within one business day".
